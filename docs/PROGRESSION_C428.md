# C428 - Current progression, acquisition and upkeep

2026-10-22. Read-only assessment of installed 0.2.64.4 and current source, with fresh default Sandbox tuning. No recipe, loot, battery, damage or skill changes are approved here. The owner's loaded global loot multiplier is 1.011111111111111, not the fresh default 1.00; saved tuning remains untouched.

## Conclusion

Progression exists, but it is a **soft set of parallel paths**, not a dependable early/mid/late unlock ladder. Skills and access to tools separate devices more than rare materials or knowledge do. Every one of the 33 OMHTT craftRecipe definitions, including packing recipes, declares needToBeLearn=false. Advanced heads do not require earlier heads. Rare modules can be acquired before a base, and there is no day or skill gate on pack rewards.

The reliably first craft by skill requirement is Concertina Wire, if barbed wire and pliers are found. Wood spikes are another early route. The last craft is character-dependent: Arc needs Electrical 4, Rotary Blade needs Mechanics 4, and Ballista requires three skills. A specific Mythic module may be the longest search, but any Mythic can arrive very early. It is not accurate to promise that Arc or a Mythic appears last for every character.

The major compression is the combination of inexpensive reusable mechanical traps, common-component head recipes, two craftable starter modules and frequent three-item packs. Acquisition is currently closer to a generous defense toolkit with upgrades than a long technology progression.

## A practical progression sequence

These are relative stages, not enforced dependencies or measured dates. Starting skills, XP multipliers/books, town, container loot, risk tolerance and Horde Night intensity can reverse or compress them.

| Stage | Likely acquisition | Actual gate and resources | What can bypass it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First supplies | Concertina Wire, then basic spikes or Barbed Panel | Pliers plus barbed wire; or Carpentry 1, knife, wood and bindings | A carpentry character can start with wooden defenses before finding wire |
| Mechanical defenses | Poke Floor and Tether Dart Trap | Mechanics 1/2, metal stock, screws and common hand tools; Tether also rope | Skilled starts can build these immediately after targeted tool/garage looting |
| First powered turret | Base plus found firearm or Potato/Scrapshot/Rotary Bat | Mechanics 2 base, charged car battery, head/ammo; craftable automatic Firing/Detection require Electrical 2 | Found modules avoid the module crafting skill/material cost; a found pistol avoids crafting a custom head |
| Coordinated setup | Controller, switches/sensors, expanded storage/range | Electrical 3 Controller; Electrical 1/2 triggers; shared power stockpile | Controller is optional for a standalone automatic turret and mechanical traps |
| Specialized heads | Ballista, Lobber, Rotary Blade and Arc | Carpentry/Mechanics/Electrical skill combinations, listed below | No prior-head or blueprint prerequisite; specialist characters may reach a preferred head first |
| Continued upgrades | Larger kits, stacked modules, Siphon/Regulator/Grid combinations | Finding the particular items, slots, power/ammo upkeep | Random packs can deliver advanced parts during the first stage |

No credible universal day-by-day estimate follows from recipe definitions alone. A low-skill survivor can spend much longer training than gathering these ingredients; a specialist can skip that delay. The conditional loot timetable below provides actual probabilities instead of inventing skill-training times.

Most electrical recipes also retain a screwdriver and pliers; head recipes can additionally need wrench, metal saw, hammer or snips. Exact tool alternatives remain in source. Alternate pump-free Potato/Scrapshot/Lobber recipes trade extra scrap/pipe for Mechanics 4, so a missing Tire Pump is not an absolute endgame gate. All of these current recipes use ordinary inventory crafting rather than a new OMHTT industrial workstation.

An illustrative **autonomous Potato turret**, making its base, pump-based head and Makeshift Firing/Detection, consumes in total:

- 3 Metal Pipes, 3 Scrap Metal, 3 Small Metal Sheets, 12 Screws.
- 7 Electronics Scrap, 3 Electric Wire, 4 Duct Tape uses, 1 Tire Pump.
- One charged Car Battery and potatoes to operate it; tools are additional, retained requirements.
- Mechanics 2 / Electrical 2. Looted modules can remove part of this bill and the module crafting prerequisite.

The corresponding **Arc setup** uses 2 Metal Pipes, 3 Scrap Metal, 4 Small Metal Sheets, 24 Screws, 13 Electronics Scrap, 6 Electric Wire and 2 Duct Tape uses, plus a charged Car Battery and retained tools. Its main extra gate is Electrical 4, not an exotic component or ammunition supply.

## Battery and ammunition logistics

- Poke Floor, Tether Dart and passive barriers need no battery or ammunition. Their durable repeated damage makes them strong early economic choices. They still have condition/repair costs; they are not literally maintenance-free.
- Turrets accept the three native Car Battery types. One usable charged battery can start a standalone turret. Native battery removal requires a screwdriver and a timed action; its template does not declare a Mechanics skill threshold. Access, installed condition and remaining charge still matter. There is no sound basis for treating every nearby car as a free full battery.
- A Controller can provide fallback power to several assigned turrets. A setup with N turrets does not necessarily require N+1 batteries. Spares extend uptime rather than unlock another technology tier.
- Current off-grid Controller idle baseline consumes one full charge per 48 game hours, before connected demand. Building power currently bypasses its battery idle cost; turrets themselves require Grid for direct grid use. This distinction is actual behavior, not a proposed change.
- Rotary Bat/Blade baseline motion budgets are 6/3 game hours per full battery, before modifiers. Their motion currently uses a different speed basis from battery accounting; the C428 timing fix is necessary before claiming reliable fast-forward endurance.
- Arc .02% base per shot gives 5,000 shots per full battery, or about 83 minutes of uninterrupted one-shot/second firing at normal speed, before modules, delays and refunds. Two ordinary Uncommon Firing/Detection modules add .5 each, for 2x total and about 42 minutes. Makeshift module costs differ. This is arithmetic, not a measured field endurance test.
- Firearms need compatible magazines/ammunition; Ballista consumes spears; Scrapshot and Lobber need their payloads. Ammo availability can be a more meaningful continuing cost than head crafting. Siphon, Regulator and Grid can substantially change battery logistics.

## Module acquisition: current default probability

Turrets-enabled defaults give each eligible dead zombie a **15% chance of one pack**. Each pack makes **three weighted selections with replacement**. There is no tier unlock based on day, skill, earlier discoveries or Horde Night wave. Repeated items are possible. Traps-only expansion loot is a separate path and does not imply the turret pack timeline.

The 33 entries sum to weight **292**. Tier anchors are 20/18/14/10/6/3/1 from Abundant to Mythic. Six entries have Mythic weight 1: Grid, Void, Synergy, Enhanced Firing, Enhanced Detection and Autoloader. [Reproducible result data](evidence/c428-loot.json).

For desired entries with combined weight W, per-kill chance of at least one is `p = 0.15 * (1 - (1 - W / 292)^3)`. Chance after N kills is `1 - (1 - p)^N`. This assumes all applicable packs are collected/opened and unchanged independent draws; it excludes additional container finds.

| Desired find | Median eligible kills | Kills for 90% chance | Median days at an illustrative 50 kills/day |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Any pack | 5 | 15 | 0.10 |
| Small Expansion Kit | 34 | 111 | 0.68 |
| Any Mythic | 77 | 254 | 1.54 |
| Siphon, or one particular other Legendary | 152 | 503 | 3.04 |
| Large Expansion Kit | 152 | 503 | 3.04 |
| One particular Mythic, such as Grid | 451 | 1,499 | 9.02 |

The day column is a conditional example, not a claim that a typical new player kills 50 zombies every day. At 10/day, multiply it by five; at 100/day, halve it. The 90% threshold for a specific Mythic is about 30 days at 50/day, while a lucky first pack can contain it.

100 eligible kills average 15 packs and 45 component selections, with roughly a 60% chance of at least one Mythic. A 300-kill Horde Night would average 45 packs and 135 selections if all corpses are eligible and looted. No separate Horde pack cap was found. These quantities can make hordes a major acceleration mechanism.

Sixteen procedural container pools also receive loose components and/or packs, including garage, mechanical, electronics, tool and firearm-accessory loot. Targeted scavenging can shorten the estimates. A procedural weight is not a direct per-container percentage; no exact container/day estimate is claimed without simulating the native roll rules and the selected loot preset.

An important controls inconsistency: positive ModuleLootRate values affect container supply, but do not scale corpse pack chance; zero disables it. PackZombieChance separately controls corpse supply. Labels and the proposed rarity controls must make this clear. Turning off the Potato head also currently affects module supply incorrectly; see the companion audit.

## Comparison with current vanilla crafting

Installed 42.21 scripts are the numerical authority for this comparison. The official [Build 42 overview](https://projectzomboid.com/blog/features-overview-build-42-20/) describes deeper long-term crafting, but does not establish acquisition dates or a mandatory balance target for this mod.

| Native reference | OMHTT comparison | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carved spear: handle/long stick plus sharp knife, no learned recipe | Basic barriers/spikes need Carpentry 1 and four wood/binding inputs | Plausible early defense progression; barriers are more expensive than one handheld spear, with a different defensive role |
| Remote Controller V1: Electrical 2, learned recipe, a remote/cordless phone/pager plus electronics and glue; higher versions use Electrical 4/6 | OMHTT Controller Electrical 3, known recipe, generic sheet/electronics/wire/screw inputs | Skill gate is credible, but knowledge and specialist-component gates are lighter than native remote technology |
| Crafted radio tiers: Electrical 1/2/3 with learned recipes | Arc requires Electrical 4, is known immediately and uses generic salvage | Arc is later by skill, but not by discovery or special parts; profession choice strongly changes arrival time |
| Native Pipe Bomb: learned recipe, 20 Gunpowder, 3 Electronics Scrap plus pipe/twine | OMHTT Makeshift Bomb: Mechanics 2 / Electrical 1, 8 Gunpowder, 1 Electronics Scrap plus pipe/scrap/tape | OMHTT ammunition has a lower acquisition burden. Different damage/radius must be compared before claiming it should cost the same |

The main concern is **under-gating advanced capability**, rather than requiring too many starter materials. A powerful reusable trap without ammo/power, a cheap generic-parts advanced head, and abundant upgrades reinforce each other. This does not prove every damage/cost is wrong; playtesting remains necessary. More expensive screws alone would mostly add scavenging repetition and would not create a clear technology ladder.

## Recommended next balance discussion

Keep basic spikes/wire accessible. Preserve useful alternative paths instead of making every player craft every head. Give advanced capabilities a small number of meaningful, reviewed skill/component/knowledge steps; compare skill-start characters as well as zero-skill survivors. Review pack supply separately, especially Horde Night kill volumes, while preserving owner LootLab changes and selected rarity assignments.

Do not nerf recipes, battery life, rarity or drops as a side effect of the settings/visual fixes. A follow-up proposal should show exact proposed versus current gates, full setup bills and pack probabilities before approval. Recommendation 3A in the [audit](AUDIT_C428.md) authorizes that design work, not unreviewed numerical implementation.

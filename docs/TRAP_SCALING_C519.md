# C519 - Endgame trap coverage and sustainable output

Owner asks how to prevent endgame bases converging on thousands of Poke Floors without simply replacing them with Concertina or another trap. Discussion only: no damage, recipes, upkeep, recovery or deployment changes are approved. C518 multiplayer/projectile/rotary and bomb-loading work remains pending separately.

## Current source evidence

- Poke defaults: 45 damage per eligible zombie, 1.5-second cycle, 200 health, 1 wear. `ControlPoke.step` charges wear once per successful cycle (`cycle.worn`), not once per damaged victim. Multiple victims admitted to one stroke therefore increase damage without additional wear. Devices have independent stroke histories; successive tiles can hit the same victim independently.
- `ControlPokeAction` restores full health using one Scrap Metal and a Wrench, with the current four-second repair profile. Poke is mechanical and does not spend a battery for each strike. At defaults, full condition supplies 200 wear-charged successful cycles absent other damage. A one-target-per-cycle illustration gives 9,000 nominal damage before overkill and target eligibility; multi-target strokes can exceed that. This is arithmetic, not a measured horde kill count.
- Poke construction already has meaningful ingredients: the pump recipe uses one Tire Pump, four Metal Bars, two Small Metal Sheets and eight Screws, plus tools and Mechanics 1. An improvised recipe also exists. Thousands of deployed traps are not established as a typical attainable endgame from this report alone.
- Concertina defaults: 10 damage per contact interval, normally two seconds. `BarbContact.step` uses one per-zombie timer across barb sections, so moving between adjacent sections does not grant each a fresh immediate damage tick. Independent wire/attachment health is 50 and wears by one per positively damaged target. Wire slows movement while touching it.
- `BarbSupport.wear` does not apply attack wear to shared-health attachments; these depend on native backing damage. This distinction must be included before claiming equal recurring costs across all trap families. It is existing design, not automatically a bug.
- Bed Spikes default to 20/50 damage, 50/100 health and six wear per confirmed damaging fall, with a five-second recovery rule. They are conditional trip/fall traps, not guaranteed damage every step.
- Poke pickup records carried health; wire packing also preserves health. There is no confirmed pickup-healing exploit from the inspected paths. Repair and recovery consistency still belongs in any balance patch's regression checks.

## Recommendation: finite output, distinct jobs, useful investment

The goal should be that large trap fields are expensive, consumable defensive depth with diminishing tactical value, rather than the universally best self-sufficient endgame. Thousands of functioning traps should remain powerful given that investment. No finite numerical nerf can guarantee an arbitrarily large field never kills a finite horde; guaranteeing that requires a hard rule or countermechanic. Do not promise wear alone eliminates every trap-only strategy.

1. **Remove Poke's crowd-efficiency subsidy.** Charge wear per successfully damaged target, with coefficients chosen against actual damage and service cost. Preserve once-per-victim stroke protection, committed-hit accounting and server authority. Extend the same sustained-output accounting review to wire, barbs, beds and barriers; do not blindly force identical coefficients onto different roles. In particular, decide how shared-health barbed attachments pay for sustained offense.
2. **Balance repair output across families.** Compare useful damage/control per unit of materials and time. Consider materials restoring a defined amount of condition rather than every repair refilling every trap for one common item. Do not balance primarily through long hold-to-repair timers or thousands of individual maintenance clicks. Convenient bounded servicing may be needed if upkeep becomes the main cost; it is not authorized by this discussion.
3. **Give traps different jobs.** Poke: strong short-range burst with limited endurance under a crowd. Wire/panels: control and attrition, lower killing efficiency; existing shared barb cadence and non-multiplying slowdown help. Beds: opportunistic trips/fall damage. Barriers: directional retaliation while exposed to attacks. Turrets: sustained ranged finishing paid for with ammo/power. Traps may still kill; a universal nonlethal floor is not proposed.
4. **Evaluate layouts, not individual DPS alone.** Compare Poke-only, wire-only, mixed beds/Poke/wire, barriers, turret-only and mixed trap/turret defenses with equal construction and replenishment budgets. Include short versus deep corridors, continuous versus burst crowds and fast versus slow crossings. Measure kills/leaks, resources spent, conditions remaining, control duration and service effort. Include different Sandbox values and single-player/faction resource scales. Exact coefficients should follow these comparisons.

Keep physical activation responsive. A balance change must not disguise the remaining multiplayer timing defects as intended activation delay. Expose meaningful wear, durability and repair tuning in the existing Traps Sandbox page; defaults and native-support interactions need owner review.

## Choices

**1A - Shared upkeep and role rebalance (recommended).** Start with per-victim Poke wear, a whole-trap damage/control-per-repair comparison and differentiated roles. No arbitrary placed-count limit, universal invulnerability timer or new corpse system. Select actual numbers after the comparison.

**1B - Add corpse obstruction as well.** Killing lanes can eventually clog or disable traps until cleared, giving dense fields a physical counter. This needs a consistent rule for relevant floor hazards, legible feedback, server/client synchronization and bounded corpse queries. It risks becoming repetitive cleanup, undermining intentional corpse barricades, or shifting the optimum to non-floor defenses. Investigate only if 1A cannot meet the desired endurance target.

**1C - Construction-cost nerf only.** Easy to explain and implement, but mainly delays accumulation; it does little to control indefinitely cheap operation after the base is built. Not recommended as the main answer.

Avoid an arbitrary global cap, unexplained temporary immunity to all traps, hidden proximity penalties, and increasing placement/repair time as the principal solution. Each can discourage valid mixed layouts or turn maintenance into the challenge instead of defense design.

No implementation, packaging, upload or new player test. **Decision needed: balancing direction.**

## C520 - Dulling, corpse obstruction and further alternatives

Owner finds corpse obstruction and higher construction costs enticing, proposes diminishing damage as durability falls with repairing restoring sharpness, and explicitly asks to keep discussing alternatives. This is interest and a proposed mechanic, not approval to implement C519 1B/1C or any numeric curve. C518 remains separate.

The inspected Poke, Barb and Bed damage paths use configured damage without a condition multiplier; Barrier's Foundation damage getter selects tier/settings without condition scaling. Rotary's ordinary damage path uses its effective stats and head condition gates operation; do not assume a new sharpening mechanic applies automatically to turret heads, firearms, blunt bats or explosive payloads.

**Dulling assessment:** a visible damage consequence before breakage makes repair tactically valuable. Prefer deriving effectiveness from existing condition, with repair restoring it, rather than introducing a second sharpness meter and separate service action. Health represents damaged or bent working parts as well as edge sharpness. Barbs that share native backing health need an explicit offensive-wear policy; the wall's remaining health is not automatically the blade's sharpness.

An illustrative curve for discussion is 100% damage at 75-100% condition; 75% damage at 50% condition; 50% damage at 25% condition or less while operational; zero at breakage. A smooth curve avoids abrupt threshold surprises. These percentages are examples, not selected defaults. A floor keeps damaged traps useful and avoids damage approaching zero long before the object breaks. Charging wear per victim while lowering damage increases wear needed per kill; calibrate those effects together.

Dulling alone can encourage larger fields to compensate for weaker individual traps. Full repairs for one common material also make the mechanic principally a servicing chore unless material restoration is balanced. More expensive construction stretches progression but does not constrain an already established field's ongoing output. Keep these limitations explicit.

### Alternatives and combinations

- **Corpse cover/obstruction:** a kill lane loses effectiveness as bodies accumulate, encouraging alternate lanes and a breach/cleanup decision. Prefer a visible grace zone and progressive obstruction before a clear jam state, rather than one body instantly switching off a trap. Physically relevant floor coverage matters, not a generic corpse radius affecting every weapon. Wire/barbed backing and elevated heads require sensible exceptions. Genuine native corpse addition/removal, carrying, decomposition, engine cleanup, persistence and multiplayer need proof; never reuse live/corpse-proxy actors as damage targets. Corpse removal should restore the unblocked state. Avoid corpse counts over the whole world every frame. This could displace dominance toward elevated defenses and must be assessed in mixed layouts.
- **Crowd load/trampling:** more zombies using a trap spend more condition; a few stragglers are cheap, a dense horde consumes the frontline. Per-successful-victim wear is the simplest starting point; extra trampling independent of hits is a separate possible mechanic. Do not introduce a permanent global trap-count penalty. This fits dulling but can create a steep failure cascade if tuned too aggressively.
- **Finite replaceable working parts:** blades/spike cartridges/springs provide a recurring supply cost while retaining the frame. It creates understandable logistics but adds items, UI, replenishment and compatibility work. It must not require individually servicing every few strikes; compare against reusing existing repair materials first.
- **Deliberate reset intervals:** Poke performs a strong attack and needs meaningful mechanical recovery. Keep first activation immediate and local presentation clear; do not call network delay a cooldown. Longer recovery alone encourages more adjacent traps, so it is not an adequate standalone answer to carpeting. Existing Tether recovery remains unrelated.
- **Construction bottlenecks/material investment:** targeted mechanical or hardened-metal ingredients can distinguish powered/repeating traps from improvised passive defenses. Cost should reflect useful output and include a fair acquisition path; avoid one rare item creating a pure loot lottery. Costs/recipes remain subject to owner approval and a progression comparison.

Suggested package to explore: condition-linked damage, restrained material-cost changes, and visible corpse obstruction for appropriate floor hazards, with per-victim Poke wear included in the endurance comparison. Do not stack every proposed penalty. Compare this package against the current equal-budget layout scenarios before selecting numbers. Expose curve strength/minimum damage and obstruction settings in the existing Traps Sandbox page if chosen; an option does not substitute for sensible defaults.

**Dulling design choices for discussion:** 1A use existing condition/Repair (recommended); 1B add independent sharpness/Sharpen maintenance (more realism, more state and chores). No selection requested as immediate implementation approval. **Decision needed: preferred combination and eventual numeric targets; discussion continues.**

## C521 - Reject maintenance-driven obstruction as the primary answer

Owner likes the idea of corpse obstruction but rejects individually hauling bodies off a normal base perimeter as tedious. They would build replacement floors elsewhere. They propose no retrigger until clear, including the death/corpse case, while recognizing that more traps can compensate. Owner explicitly requests candid validation and simulation rather than agreement; discussion continues, with no gameplay approval.

**Revised recommendation:** withdraw C520's preferred corpse-obstruction package as the main solution. It turns successful defense into cleanup labor and can increase the incentive for spare rows. Dulling has the same risk if efficient servicing is absent. A normal perimeter should remain a legitimate use case; distinguish coverage from indefinitely cheap unattended killing.

### Executed design model

`tools/tests/trap_layout_model_c521.py` runs 40 deterministic cases. [Results and assumptions](evidence/c521-layout-model.json). Ten identical lanes, ten zombies per lane, 100 HP, 45 damage, one tile/second, arrivals two seconds apart; depths 1/3/6/12; either immediate passage or eight seconds held at the final tile. Five rules: repeating strokes, rearm after living occupancy clears, corpse blocking, rearm after both living/corpse occupancy clears, and six-second automatic obstruction clearing. This is an illustrative discrete-time layout model, **not an engine, AI, multiplayer or balanced-gameplay simulation**. Instant group strokes omit Poke's 150ms admission window; no trips, armor, slows, durability, resources, player damage, obstruction geometry or pathfinding. Varying depth changes investment, so this is a workaround test, not an equal-budget ranking.

For uninterrupted crossings, normal repeat and live-only reset both kill 100/100 with three rows. Requiring both living/corpse clearance kills 10 with three rows, 40 with six and 100 with twelve, leaving progressively more corpse-blocked tiles. Six-second automatic clearing kills 40 with three rows but 100 with six. With an eight-second hold, one repeating row kills all while live-only reset kills none; three fresh rows restore all kills. Thus clear-before-reset can prevent standing-target farming but does not solve coverage scaling. The first manual expectation for six permanently blocked rows was 20 kills; the model correctly gives 40 because survivors retain damage from the first two working rows. The assertion was corrected after examining that mechanism, not the model changed to force the expected answer.

This supports the owner's workaround concern under these assumptions. It does not prove precise in-game counts or that every possible corpse design fails. The initial claim of 100 hauling actions is also not universal: body placement and clearing behavior matter, but task burden still tends to grow with successful kills.

### Fresh directions to explore

**1A - Resource expenditure with centralized automatic servicing (recommended for further evaluation).** Let stored repair materials replenish eligible traps through a Controller or service station, preferably during quiet periods. Each trap's useful damage/control consumes condition/material value; individual dragging/repair clicking is removed. Materials are still acquired and replenished centrally. More deployed tiles improve coverage and capacity but do not make each kill's repair cost disappear. At the toy model's 45 damage/100 HP, 100 kills require 300 successful contacts whether there are three or twelve rows; a per-contact expenditure follows work done, unlike one-wear-per-multi-target-cycle. This is illustrative cost accounting, not selected material pricing. All damaging passive families, including shared-health barbed attachments, need comparable accounting so Concertina does not become the free substitute. Idle coverage should not accrue pointless rent. Risks: automation can become another mandatory component; resource balancing may merely shift farming; native inventory authorization and bounded repairs must be proven; automatic remote repairs must remain legible. This is new design scope, not an already supported Controller capability. No free/offline instant repairs, invented resource numbers or new consumable item are approved.

**1B - Active grouped defense instead of cleanup.** A player chooses when to discharge/rearm a bank through a lever/controller, with visible readiness and automatic mechanical recovery. One meaningful group action replaces many corpse actions. This trades passive autonomy for timing/crowd management. More banks or automation can bypass the attention constraint unless designed carefully; it changes the user's autonomous-base fantasy and would not automatically apply sensibly to passive barbed wire. Treat as an optional active defense direction, not a disguised fix to every existing pressure trap.

**Additional role adjustment: movement-driven damage.** Poke acts on a new eligible crossing, barbs scratch while traversed, and neither farms indefinitely from stationary occupancy; control effects can remain. This better distinguishes perimeter softening from ranged finishing, but the model proves more rows still kill. It is useful only if the goal permits expensive trap-only depth; it cannot be sold as an anti-carpet solution by itself. Any exact repeat/rearm rules need separate review.

**Discard as primary answers:** automatic corpse expiry alone (a local cooldown that extra rows bypass), larger recipe costs alone (delays accumulation), dulling alone (invites compensating rows), and maintenance based chiefly on individual timed clicks. Adversary-specific counters, new enemy AI and artificial global placed-count penalties add major compatibility/design costs and are not recommended merely to rescue the obstruction idea.

A finite-cost, independently repeatable killing device scales when players build more of it. To constrain that growth meaningfully, choose a cost in resources, attention, space, or explicit limits. The owner has rejected excessive maintenance labor; that does not imply resource costs are unwanted. Prefer making a large defensive field a paid playstyle rather than forbidding it. Next discussion should choose between a logistics/automation tradeoff and active combat attention, not approve implementation or numbers. Preserve C518 work separately. **Decision needed; discussion continues.**
